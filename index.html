How it works

  1. Your image is shrunk to at most 240 px wide in the browser, so extraction stays instant even on a 20-megapixel photo.
  2. Median-cut quantisation splits the pixels into groups by their widest colour channel, again and again, until it has the number of colours you asked for. Each swatch is the average colour of its group, weighted by how many pixels fell in it.
  3. Harmonies are pure hue maths — complementary is the base hue +180°, analogous ±30°, triadic +120°/+240°, split-complementary +150°/+210°.
  4. Nothing leaves your device. No upload, no server, no account. Close the tab and the images are gone; only the palette list in localStorage stays, and you can clear it above.

Honest limits

  • Extraction is approximate. Colours are quantised from a downscaled copy of your image, so a swatch is the most common colour of a colour family, not necessarily one exact pixel from the original. Near-pure colours are snapped to the pure value (so flat red reads #FF0000, not #FE0001), but a photo's in-between shades stay as they are.
  • Very small images give few colours. A 32×32 icon may only hold two or three distinct colours — asking for ten will not invent more.
  • Gradients give many similar shades. A smooth sky gradient quantises into a row of near-identical blues. Sort by hue or raise the saturation floor if that bothers you.
  • Near-white and near-black are ignored by default. That keeps a white border or a black background from swallowing the palette — uncheck the box if you genuinely want them.
  • Harmonies are computed, not designed. The maths is exact, but taste is yours: a complementary pair can still clash in a real design.
